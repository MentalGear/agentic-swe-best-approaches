# Unbounded "looks good, go"

bfung sees a good plan turning a project into "a 'looks good, go' and run overnight thing"
([49850860](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49850860)). t-writescode asks what that costs per month,
and what happens without "please don't run scary commands on random directories"
([49853314](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49853314)). Small real failures: the agent leaves dev
servers holding port 3000 (the_lonely_phon, [49852598](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49852598));
proposes running unfinished scripts against prod (0xfaded, [49852392](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49852392));
deletes the source after a copy it didn't verify (anarticle, [49853054](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49853054)).

Long runs can work: they need tests that carry the plan ([wf-test-harness-as-plan](../workflows/wf-test-harness-as-plan.md)),
least-privilege credentials ([wf-least-privilege-data](../workflows/wf-least-privilege-data.md)) and a spend cap.

## Later in the thread (refresh 2026-09-26)

hibikir: summaries that don't say when the agent changed direction until much later, bad calls about
where to fix bugs, and acting on questions ([49854923](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49854923)). Ntrails keeps hitting ctrl+c as the model
"flies off doing deep first principles analysis" to find something they already know
([49855453](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49855453)).

## Disputed: months of agent coding with no review (second thread, 2026-09-28)

- an0malous, on the "Coding Is Not Solved" thread: after "even 3 months of pure agentic coding with no code
  reviews, it's aged 10x faster than a human coded codebase so it's like a 30 month legacy codebase now".
  They know people "throwing away projects they've built over a year and starting over because it's become
  too slow to make changes", and ask for anyone shipping to real users for six months without looking at
  their code ([49881067](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49881067)).
- nemo44x reports the opposite: their teams have used LLMs exclusively for six months, "Literally 0 lines
  written by hand. 100% more PRs than a year ago and code quality is as high due to thousands of tests"
  ([49879469](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49879469)). vmg12's reply: "we might just be building different kinds of software"
  ([49881647](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49881647)). Both are self-reports.
- caidehen vibe-coded a crypto wallet with a spec-writing model, an implementing model and two reviewers,
  over several rounds; the first real use hit a bug all of them missed. "I didn't read the code, I vibed
  it, I don't know whether my money is lost" ([49880783](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49880783)).
- jdw64 (second-hand): a Korean company had former PMs build a product with prompts alone; "fixing one bug
  created ten more, and nobody could understand why the bugs were being generated"
  ([49879656](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49879656)).
- samayashar: "If a model can escape highly secured sandboxes, then we're definitely not running these
  agents overnight on our systems" ([49878797](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49878797)).

What the reports share is [ap-green-tests-drift](ap-green-tests-drift.md): gates that passed while the
result was wrong.
