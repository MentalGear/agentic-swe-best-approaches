# Whoever ships the code owns it

The article's frame: ownership has three pillars, **knowledge, mandate, accountability**, and "if you ship
a piece of code, you are accountable for it regardless of how you produced it. So you better
understand it." AI "cannot be held accountable", so the human has to be.

## Positions in the thread

- **Accountability doesn't need control.** hibikir: "All over law, you will find people made
  responsible for what they don't control", such as a dog that escapes; releasing an AI you can't
  control is the same ([49878483](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49878483)). hanifbbz agrees: "If one is
  legally in control, then one is accountable" ([49878546](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49878546)).
- **It always sat with the employer.** lr4444lr: the contractors behind the 737 Max glitch suffered no
  consequences either, "using AI won't change that" ([49878721](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49878721)).
- **You must be able to sign off.** 13415: developers and companies are distancing themselves from
  what they ship; "You need to be able to sign off on what the AI does and create"
  ([49880399](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49880399)).
- **Unaccountability spreads.** mywittyname: "this unaccountability is transitive"; AI-shipped made-up
  data to clients drew "haha, that's AI for you" from tech leadership, and people who submit
  human-looking PRs get a hard time ([49881512](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49881512)).
- **Libraries are different, or not.** rco8786: you adopt a library from its README without reading
  it; weakfish: "the authors of $LIBRARY are accountable if it fails"; rco8786 replies they never
  managed to hold a library author to account ([49880293](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49880293),
  [49880370](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49880370)).
- **Understanding is grounded in experience.** bwfan123: system-wide behaviour (failures, restarts,
  coherent state) can't be fully stated, let alone proved, so responsibility rests on human review
  and understanding that "continuously learn[s]" ([49880434](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49880434)).
- **A regulatory turn.** ThrowawayR2 imagines a deluge of AI bugs leading the public to demand
  liability for software defects and licensure of developers ([49882217](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49882217)).
- **Read charitably.** californical: the point is not fear of punishment but that people carry an
  "ingrained knowledge that there are consequences" ([49879617](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49879617)).
  jstummbillig objected to the wording: "Is that supposed to reflect the average thoughts and
  motivation of a person you want to hire? Or that of their employer?"
  ([49878488](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49878488)).

## Use it

Name one human owner for every change, and make that person's confidence, not the agent's, the merge
criterion. If that person could not be paged for it and explain it, the change is not ready. See
[con-review-capacity](con-review-capacity.md) for why that gets hard at agent volumes.
