# Skip the plan, review the PR

Syntaf's arithmetic for ten sessions a day ([49857655](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49857655)):

- Review 10 plans, *then* 10 PRs, adjust 1–2, merge 8; or
- Review 10 PRs, adjust maybe 4, merge 6.

"The first option just feels like needless attention for the sake of feeling in control." It works for
them because "our codebase has enough linters, hooks, and guards such that generally the agent just
follows our blessed patterns already … I'm still going to read the code anyways."

## Related reports

- samdjstephens stopped using plan mode because they "usually ended up mentally glossing over the
  generated plans anyway", and the clarifying questions were things the model would have resolved
  itself. Their replacement: understand the system well enough *yourself* to predict the model's
  assumptions, and plan the prompt ([49856922](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49856922)).
- jaapz: "9 times out of 10 I just say … xyz is the problem, fix it", in a codebase of a few hundred
  thousand lines that shows how things are done; writing plan files for another session to rebuild
  context "didn't result in better output" ([49855652](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49855652)).
- astrostl thinks plan mode steers people toward one-shot big-bang changes and "assuming the plan was
  sufficiently comprehensive because it seemed to ask reasonable questions"
  ([49858230](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49858230)).

## Preconditions

A codebase with established patterns to copy; linters, tests and hooks that reject what's off-pattern;
reversible changes. Without those, see [con-blast-radius](../concepts/con-blast-radius.md).
btreecat's reply to jaapz: without a plan, "it always does something unexpected and undesirable"
([49856059](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49856059)).
