# Article: Coding is NOT solved

- **Author:** Alex Ewerlöf (Substack byline). In the HN thread the account `hanifbbz` introduced itself
  as "Author here"; I did not verify how that handle maps to the byline.
- **Published:** 2026-09-26 (date shown on the page); HN title "Coding Is Not Solved"
- **URL:** <https://blog.alexewerlof.com/p/coding-is-not-solved>

A polemic, not a study: the author says it mixes referenced claims with "the result of my own
experience", and the thread's most common criticism was that it offers no measurements. What it
claims, in the author's words or close to them:

## Core claims

1. **Reading the code is not optional for software that matters.** Code you may skip reading:
   personal software, proofs of concept, and deliberately weaponised AI. Software that has engineers
   on payroll (healthcare, finance, automotive, defence, power, aviation, manufacturing) has low
   risk tolerance and needs accountability.
2. **AI cannot be held accountable**, and "you cannot be responsible for what you can't control".
   Ownership has three pillars: knowledge, mandate, accountability. "You cannot be accountable for
   what you don't understand." See [con-accountability](../concepts/con-accountability.md).
3. **LLMs succeed at code because of feedback loops**: compilers, tests and harnesses feed errors
   back until "most errors are solved or hidden". The models themselves are "stochastic and
   probabilistic" and struggle with logic and volume.
4. **Volume defeats review.** "Run multiple-agents in parallel! The sheer volume of code makes it
   humanly impossible/expensive to review and you give up", so it is merged based on "trust" (his scare quotes).
   See [con-review-capacity](../concepts/con-review-capacity.md).
5. **Fallacies he lists:** a full spec can be written upfront; "English is the new programming
   language" (natural language is vague and conflicting, which is why programming languages exist);
   "I move much faster" (measure service levels, not SLOC or PR count); "agent is the new
   compiler"; "the leverage has shifted to taste"; "AI is an equalizer" (he says a multiplier).
6. **Code is a side effect of thinking.** Good engineers work out the why before the how, and code
   "communicates the committed state of a solution", not the struggle behind it. See
   [con-coding-vs-engineering](../concepts/con-coding-vs-engineering.md).
7. **Economics:** even at 1000x faster and 100x cheaper, "slow is fast" only pays for
   low-risk-tolerance software. Vendors sell SLAs; he questions the total cost of ownership of
   prompting your own replacement for a SaaS product.
8. **Skill decay:** "AI overdose" puts an expiry date on your skills; keep an AI-free hobby project.

## Examples he uses

- An agent asked to update five npm dependencies, all patch releases, took 12 minutes and 72 steps;
  he says he could do it "in less than a minute" (his own experiment).
- Three Claude Code bug reports (Bun help menu shown instead of Claude Code, installer deleting
  itself, extra usage charged despite plan capacity), cited as evidence about the "coding is
  solved" camp. Links are in the article.
- Shopify's CEO first mandating AI use (2025) and later calling the result "slop grenades"
  (2026, as quoted from Business Insider in the article).

## What he predicts

Engineers split into technical product managers (POCs, then hand-over), AI managers, AI deployment
engineers, and AI quality engineers.

## Reading notes

- The article is openly hostile to named people and companies; those passages are recorded here only
  as context, not as findings.
- Claims about vendors' motives and about Claude users were not evaluated.
- The article names no model versions; the thread's counter-argument that newer models change this (lbrito paraphrases it as "you need to
  try the latest model", [49881111](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49881111)) is discussed in [src-hn-coding-not-solved](src-hn-coding-not-solved.md).
